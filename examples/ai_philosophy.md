John Searle's Chinese Room argument, published in 1980, proves that no computer program can ever understand language. The Turing test, proposed by Alan Turing in 1950, is widely accepted by philosophers as a sufficient test for consciousness. Large language models are just stochastic parrots that cannot reason. Since LLMs are trained only to predict the next token, they cannot have internal world models. The hard problem of consciousness was formulated by David Chalmers in the 1990s.
